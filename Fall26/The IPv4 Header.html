5-minute lab: build an IP packet with Scapy

Make an empty IP header and look at it

Fields showing None (ihl, len, chksum) are calculated when the packet is built.

1 min
pkt = IP()
pkt.show()

Set the fields you learned

1 min
pkt = IP(dst="8.8.8.8", ttl=10, id=1234, flags="DF", proto=1)
pkt.show()

Build it and see the raw bytes

Scapy now fills in ihl, len and chksum. Look at the very first byte of the hexdump.

1 min
built = IP(raw(pkt))
built.show()
hexdump(pkt)
Why is the first byte 45?

4 = Version (IPv4), 5 = IHL (5 × 4 = 20-byte header). Two fields packed into one byte.

Add ICMP on top and send it

The / stacks layers. sr1 sends and waits for one reply.

1 min
reply = sr1(IP(dst="8.8.8.8")/ICMP(), timeout=2)
reply.show()

Check: reply src is 8.8.8.8, proto is 1 (ICMP), and its ttl is lower than it started.

Challenge: make the packet die at the first router

30 s
r = sr1(IP(dst="8.8.8.8", ttl=1)/ICMP(), timeout=2)
r.src, r[ICMP].type
What should you see?

r.src is your router's IP, and ICMP type 11 (Time Exceeded). TTL hit 0, so the router dropped the packet and told you. This is exactly how traceroute works.
